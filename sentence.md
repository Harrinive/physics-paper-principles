# Sentence-level principles

**For agents:** Start with [SKILL.md](SKILL.md). Open for any user-facing
physics or mathematics sentence or local passage.

Check every sentence under review against all 16 principles. A principle may be
inapplicable because its stated object is absent; it may not be skipped because
the editor is concentrating on another problem. Repair local problems identified
by direct reading or, when applicable, by the physics spine, terminology-and-
notation delta, object audit, or math delta. In conversation mode, apply the
principles silently; do not enumerate all 16 or emit `N/A` fields. Report
diagnostics only when the user asks for an editorial assessment.

These are drafting rules, not a process. How an editing job routes review,
records evidence, and repairs violations lives in **`physics-paper-editing`**.
That workflow must enforce every applicable principle even when it records only
the failures.

**Detect** is how to see a violation. Several principles share one artifact on purpose.

**Read as the human reader.** An agent reads with full recall: it holds every
definition, the object ledger, and the vocabulary registry at once, so a dense
sentence never makes it stall. A human reads once, in order, and remembers only
the few objects the text has made central. Judge principles 3, 13, and 16 as
that reader, not by whether an agent can parse the sentence.

| 14 | **Every clause must earn its place** — reject a redundant type gloss, a count already implied by the name or notation, or a tautological identity that adds no orientation, contrast, limitation, or consequence. Keep a concise first-use gloss when it helps the reader identify an unfamiliar object's category or role. Rewrite a redundant clause as the supported point it was gesturing at, or delete it when adjacent text already supplies that point. Do not invent a scientific point absent from the sentence or its immediate neighbors. Distinct from 5 (redundant flow between sentences), 6 (unidiomatic English that still asserts something), and 12 (prose that belongs in math). | **Delete-the-clause:** remove the clause provisionally. | 15 | **Preserve standard meaning of terminology** — keep a field-standard term's established meaning. A broader or narrower use is allowed only as a genuine generalization or specialization: the standard meaning is a special case of the new one, or the new use is a special case of the standard; the extra or restricted cases are stated at first introduction (this sentence or an earlier established definition); and a reader who knows only the standard meaning will not take a false reading. If the intended object is not that kind of extension, do not reuse the bare term — qualify it, or coin and define (principle 3). Distinct from 3 (new or undefined labels) and from math Type 1 (silent change of a definition already given in the current context). | **Standard-meaning substitution:** for each field-standard technical term, replace it with its established definition. If the sentence becomes false, or both a standard reading and a local reading remain, fail unless a genuine generalization or specialization has already been declared as in the principle. Ordinary English and locally coined terms (principle 3) are N/A. | Kernel (flag shifted standard terms) |
| 16 | **Limit reader load** — a sentence carries one main idea that a human can follow on a single reading in order. Put at most one condition or qualifier block before the main clause, do not nest a relative clause inside another, and keep small the number of technical objects the reader must recall from elsewhere. When a correct sentence is overloaded, split it, or give a condition its own sentence, rather than add a qualifier; precision is not a reason to keep the overload. When a claim needs a qualifier to stay true, prefer stating it at a level that is true without one (what the object measures or does) and leave the exact statement to where the object is defined. Distinct from 6 (unidiomatic or compressed wording), 13 (order), and 14 (a clause that adds nothing). | **Cold restatement:** using only the passage and the text before it, restate the sentence in plain words for a physicist outside the subfield. Fail when the restatement needs more than about three lookups, meets more than one condition before the main clause or more than one qualifier block after it, passes through a relative clause nested in another, comes out different from the intended claim, or comes out clearer than the sentence itself. | Load record |

### Principle 13 — tiers

- **Tier 1 — deferred & flagged:** the confusion is acknowledged in place and explicitly promised a later resolution (e.g. “⟨confusing part⟩. We show this in the following paragraph.”). Low harm; acceptable as is, but prefer a rearrangement that removes the confusion *if it does not disturb the narrative*.
- **Tier 2 — resolved next sentence:** the confusion is cleared immediately afterward (e.g. an undefined symbol followed by a long explanation instead of a short `where` clause; or a high-level/unmotivated claim followed by its justification). Mild — prefer a short `where`-clause, reorder so definition/motivation precedes use, or compress.
- **Tier 3 — never resolved / resolved much later:** the reader is left hanging or the resolution appears far away. Harmful — define or motivate before use, or move the resolution adjacent.
